# Laptops

**Status:** DRAFT
**Purpose:** The laptop categories, what separates one category from another, and a few example model lines per brand, so a salesman or an AI can place an enquiry in the right category.
**Sources:** general industry knowledge of the Dell, HP and Lenovo ranges, not Zeronix's own price or stock data. The owner has not yet confirmed which lines Zeronix sells. Brand line names change, so always check the current name before quoting.

## The categories

| Category | Who it is for | Main difference |
|---|---|---|
| Business laptop | Companies that buy many and manage them | Built for work: sturdier build, stronger security and management features, business warranty options, and the same model stays available for a long time |
| Small-business / value business laptop | Small companies that want business features at a lower price | A cut-down business line: fewer premium features than the main business line, lower price |
| Consumer laptop | Home users and students | Cheaper and more colourful, focused on looks, screen and multimedia; lighter security and management features |
| Premium ultraportable | Executives, creators, people who travel | Thin, light, high-quality screen and materials, long battery; can be sold as business or consumer |
| Gaming laptop | Gamers and some creators | Powerful graphics card, fast screen, stronger cooling; heavier and shorter battery life |
| Mobile workstation | Engineers, designers, video and 3D users | A laptop built to run professional software all day: certified graphics, more memory and storage, strongest build |
| 2-in-1 (convertible) | People who write or draw on the screen | Screen folds or detaches into a tablet; exists in both business and consumer ranges |

## Business laptop versus consumer laptop (the difference a customer feels)

| | Business laptop | Consumer laptop |
|---|---|---|
| Build | Tested for drops, dust and heat, sturdier hinges, spill-resistant keyboard on many models | Lighter build, more design variety |
| Security | Usually a TPM chip, fingerprint or face login, smart-card option, webcam shutter, remote-management options on many models | Basic security; fewer options |
| Management | Built to be set up and managed across a fleet of computers; the same model and parts stay available for a long time | Not aimed at fleets; models change often |
| Software | Usually Windows Pro | Often Windows Home |
| Warranty | Options to extend, upgrade to on-site or next-business-day service | Usually a basic warranty with fewer upgrade options |
| Ports and docking | More ports (often Ethernet) and docking support | Fewer ports |
| Price | Higher for the same processor and memory | Lower |

Rule of thumb: if the buyer is a company, needs many identical units, or cares about security and support, offer business. If the buyer is one person who wants the best screen and look for the price, offer consumer.

## Example model lines by brand

Names below are product lines (families), not single models. Each line has many models with different processors, memory and sizes.

| Business laptop | Latitude | EliteBook | ThinkPad (T, X and L series) |
| Small-business / value business | Vostro | ProBook | ThinkBook |
| Consumer laptop | Inspiron | HP consumer lines (Pavilion, and OmniBook in newer ranges) | IdeaPad |
| Premium ultraportable | XPS | Spectre, EliteBook Ultra / Dragonfly (premium business) | Yoga, ThinkPad X1 |
| Gaming laptop | Alienware, Dell G series | OMEN, Victus | Legion, LOQ |
| Mobile workstation | Precision | ZBook | ThinkPad P series |
| 2-in-1 | Latitude 2-in-1, Inspiron 2-in-1 | EliteBook x360, Pavilion x360, Spectre x360 | ThinkPad X1 2-in-1, Yoga |

Check before quoting: brands rename lines from time to time (for example Dell has been introducing "Dell Pro" and "Dell Pro Max" names for its business and workstation ranges, and HP has been moving consumer lines to "OmniBook"). If a supplier quotes a name not in this table, do not guess the category; ask.

## How to place an enquiry

1. Ask who it is for (a company or one person) and how many units.
2. Ask the work it must do (office work, design or video, gaming, on-site field use).
3. Ask about must-haves: weight, battery, screen size, security, warranty, and the budget.
4. Then choose the category from the table, and only then look at supplier prices.
