# Choosing the right category

**Status:** DRAFT
**Purpose:** A quick guide to move from what the customer says to the right product category, so the salesman or an AI asks the right questions before looking for prices.
**Sources:** general industry knowledge, plus the pages laptops, desktops and workstations in this folder. Not confirmed by the owner.

## From the customer's words to a category

| The customer says | Likely category | Ask next |
|---|---|---|
| "Laptops for my staff", "for the office" | Business laptop | How many, security needs, warranty, budget per unit |
| "Cheap laptop for home or study" | Consumer laptop | Budget, screen size, main use |
| "Laptop for gaming" | Gaming laptop | Games or software, budget |
| "Laptop for AutoCAD / 3D / video editing" | Mobile workstation | Exact software and project size |
| "Computers for reception or a small shop" | Small-business desktop, all-in-one or mini PC | Space, how many, screens needed |
| "Computers for an office of many users" | Business desktop | How many, size (tower, small or all-in-one), management needs |
| "Machine for rendering, simulation or AI" | Desktop workstation (mid or high-end) | Software, project size, memory and graphics requirement |

## Rules

1. Place the enquiry in a category first. Only then look at supplier prices.
2. Do not assume a specification the customer did not state (memory, storage, processor generation, warranty). Missing details stay unknown and are asked for.
3. If two categories fit, offer both and say the main difference (for example business versus consumer: build, security, management and warranty).
4. If the supplier's model name is not in the tables, do not guess its category; ask a person.
